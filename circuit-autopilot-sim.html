Flight model

The autopilot's control laws fly it through the real dynamics: phugoid, Dutch roll, adverse yaw, propeller torque, gear loads. It runs in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly.

Autopilot

Pitch-for-speed climb, pitch-for-path approach with throttle on speed. Below 300 ft a stabilised-approach gate triggers a go-around after 2 s outside limits. In a crosswind the approach is flown crabbed, then below 60 ft rudder aligns the nose and the into-wind wing goes down. The flare holds off just above the runway while the speed bleeds to 1.05 × the stall speed (plus ¾ of any crosswind), so the wheels touch slow and nose-up. Turbulence is measured from the wind estimate (GPS minus air data): it sets the gust margin on the approach speed, softens the short-field flare, and adds power in the flare if a lull drops the speed below the touchdown speed. Every command is rate-limited, so no mode change steps a control.

Short-field technique

Brakes held until full power, rotate at 48 kt with 10° pitch, best-angle climb at 57 kt to 100 ft. Steep 5.5° power-on approach at 60 kt aimed 40 m past the threshold, firm touchdown without float, flaps up and full braking. The 600 m and 450 m strips have 50 ft trees 150 m beyond each end; the landing check scores distances and tree clearance.

Engine failure

On the runway it rejects; below 700 ft after take-off it lands ahead; higher up it plans a power-off path to runway 09 or 27: a turn, a straight and a turn (a Dubins path at the 45° bank it flies), joining the centreline anywhere from 1.2 km out to over the runway, with the touchdown anywhere that leaves room to stop. Height for straights and turns comes from the model's own trimmed glide; the wind is the autopilot's estimate. It re-checks every second along the path it is flying and lands ahead only if no plan reaches. On final one energy decision picks flap, a slip or more path. Lined up on final it always goes for the runway. Failing the engine drops the speed to 1×. Random state starts in the air anywhere within 5 km, possibly upset: it recovers (wings level the short way, unloading first if near the stall, nose to the horizon), then flies a powered Dubins path to a final fix sized so the height can be lost on the way, and lands; with the engine out it glides to whichever runway it can reach; if far too high it first glides to the field and orbits down beside the runway, where either end stays within easy reach. Each landing scores touchdown-accuracy points: 100 on the planned touchdown point, one less per 3 m. The High circuit keeps the runway within glide reach for longer.
